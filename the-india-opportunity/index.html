The India Opportunity

India is the largest open digital market on earth and, for the first time, its enterprises are buying software and AI at prices that make a serious business. This page is the case as we actually make it to partners, the size of the prize, the evidence behind it, and the counter-evidence we think you should weigh before you commit.

Volume versus margin

India is where the world’s labs buy scale, not margin

Every major AI company treats India as a top-one or top-two market by usage. Almost none of them monetise it like one yet. That asymmetry is the opening, and the warning.

The monetisation test begins now, as free credits expire. For a partner, that means the winning move is not to chase consumer scale. It is to sell to the buyers who already have budgets, enterprises and capability centres, while attention is cheap and incumbency is unsettled.

Beyond revenue

Four compounding advantages of being here

A ready-made enterprise beachhead

More than 1,700 global capability centres, $64.6B of revenue and 1.9M staff, headed past $100B by 2030, global buyers with local budgets and global standards.

The world’s toughest product lab

A billion users, 22 official languages, voice-first behaviour and hard price ceilings. A product hardened in India travels to every emerging market.

Distribution rails no other market has

India Stack, real-time payments at tens of billions of transactions a month, digital identity and open commerce protocols, collapses onboarding and payment friction.

A strategic hedge and a talent engine

China is effectively closed. India is the only open billion-user market, with the developer depth to staff engineering as well as buy it.

Keeping ourselves honest

What we tell partners before they commit

We would rather lose a partnership at the diligence stage than lose two years in the market. Here is the case against, as we understand it.

The risks

Why an India entry fails

  • US price points do not travel. A $20-30 per seat product meets a market anchored near $5. Unit economics have to be rebuilt, not translated.
  • Free is the competitor. Global platforms and telecom operators bundle capable AI at zero rupees. A standalone paid product has to beat bundled free.
  • The window may be short. Most Indian AI startups are past first revenue by seed stage, and local teams iterate on Indic language, voice and locally priced experiences faster than imports adapt.
  • Policy leans local. Sovereign-technology sentiment, data-residency rules and cheap open-weight models from elsewhere all erode any imported-product premium.

The delivery reality

What selling here actually takes

  • Roughly twice the customisation asks of other markets. Indian buyers expect the product to be bent toward them as part of the sale.
  • Vendor-led implementation is the entry ticket. Around three-quarters of Indian enterprises have hit implementation overruns, averaging a 57% schedule slip and 43% cost escalation.
  • Professional services run heavy. Healthy software businesses keep services under about 15% of revenue; India-focused sellers routinely run above that, so revenue per employee is not comparable to the US.
  • The high-touch buyer is not universal. Capability centres and digital natives buy self-serve, much like US customers, and heavy discounting backfires even here.
Nobody has industrialised high-touch delivery for Indian enterprises, because Indian IT was built for export, not for home. In the last full year measured, exports were about 79% of a $282.6B industry against 21% domestic. Margin arbitrage, decades of export-oriented policy and government-heavy domestic demand all pointed the majors outward. The delivery muscle a modern product needs at home was never built. That vacuum is our opening, and it is closing, with domestic growth now outpacing exports.

Sources: Blume Ventures enterprise software guide; IDC-Zoho implementation study; NASSCOM Strategic Review 2025 and 2026; Lightspeed and SaaSBoomi revenue-per-employee analyses; US Trade.gov India Country Commercial Guide.

How to play

Four moves that follow from the evidence

01

Pick the right wedge

Enterprise and prosumer categories where free bundles do not reach. Consumer is defended by zero-rupee telco and platform bundles. Capability centres are the first thousand customers.

02

Re-price for India

Rebuild the economics around local price logic and usage-based pricing rather than translating a US price list. If revenue per user is lower, acquisition and serving costs have to be too.

03

Ship with a services layer

Implementation, training and support are the entry ticket, not a distraction. Productise the hand-holding instead of fighting the expectation.

04

Enter with a local partner

Distribution, entity structure for local-content rules, and enterprise relationships. This is precisely the gap the Indian IT majors left open at home, and precisely what we do.

Watch items we track for partners: the 2026-27 monetisation window as free credits expire; local application-layer startups scaling; and data-residency and sovereign-technology policy.
